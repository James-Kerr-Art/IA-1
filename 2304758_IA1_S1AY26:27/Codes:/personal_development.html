The Richest Man in Babylon - George S. Clason

The Richest Man in Babylon published in 1926 is a 100 year old book written by George S. Clason involves a set of parables regarding the attainment of wealth spoken through the lens of the man named Arkad, referred to as “The Richest man in Babylon” , along with his journey to attaining wealth as well as his success and the rules he followed to execute the whole process. Reviewing chapters such as the “7 Cures for a Lean Purse” and “5 Laws of Gold” exploring Arkad’s method of attaining wealth as well as protecting it.

7 cures for a lean purse delves into the kingdom of Babylon and how it’s people attained wealth through wisdom. After the good king Sargon returned to Babylon after a victorious battle, he noted with all the improvements made that the people were unable to support themselves financially with much of the gold landing in the pockets of a few now very wealthy men. The king was baffled at how most people ended up losing money while only very few were able to save it so he sought for the richest man in Babylon (Arkad) to understand and have him later share his wisdom with the people of Babylon so Babylon could become an even greater richer nation.

5 laws of Gold, the Old Kalabab questioned “if thou hadst thy choice; which wouldst thou choose?" Many responded Gold! Gold! Gold! He silenced them then compared these people to wild dogs starving, hungry for gold, yet when fed with wisdom to attain it along with some gold, they were likely to ignore the wisdom and waste the gold and by tomorrow they will mourn their lack and loss of gold. So he states that Gold is reserved for those who abide by its laws and by those laws one may accumulate gold.

Later on Nomasir told the tale to his son but he wanted to make sure his son knew how to manage gold before letting him run his business so he shares the table carved with the five laws of gold engraved onto it which states as follows on page 65 but to simplify. Gold comes to those who buy an estate, gold comes to those who employ others to attain it, gold clings to those who invest it under the guidance of someone experienced, Gold slips from those who invest in random businesses, Gold will flee from those who gamble it expecting earnings they can only imagine.

Personally I wish I read this book when I was younger for I ended up learning a lot of these lessons the hard way, A lot of what is implied in the book through story I had already applied except for areas regarding strict guidance, some to oversee me, to aid me to help me not only manage money but also where to invest it and when. Understanding how to manage my money early on would have been a great help but I learned from my mistakes, I only have one way to go and that is up and applying the knowledge I attained from this book I can do just that.
